Privacy Policy

Last updated 11 November 2026

Browsa doesn't collect your personal data. It has no accounts, no analytics and no advertising, and we can't see what you browse. Your browsing data stays on your device.

Browsa is a web browser for iPhone, iPad and Mac, made by Joshua Orr. Like any browser, it has to contact other services to work. This policy describes what stays on your device, what leaves it, and how to control that. Most of it applies to both apps, and the extra things the Mac app does are in Browsa for Mac. Browsa is open source, so you can check all of this in the code.

What we collect

Nothing that identifies you. We don't run servers that receive your browsing, your searches or anything you type. The app contains no analytics, advertising or crash-reporting code from us or anyone else.

On iPhone and iPad, if you choose to share diagnostics with app developers in your device's settings, Apple may give us anonymous crash reports and usage statistics. Apple handles this under its own privacy policy, and you can turn it off in Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements.

What stays on your device

  • Your spaces, tabs, favourites, saved pages and settings, including any Brave Search API key you enter
  • Your browsing history, which suggestions use to rank results
  • Thumbnails of your tabs
  • Files you download, which you can see in the Files app
  • Cookies and other data that websites store, and your answers when a site asks for a permission

We never receive any of this. It's included in your device's backups if you back up to iCloud or a computer, under the terms you set up with Apple.

Blocking and link cleaning

Ad and tracker blocking, declining cookie banners and removing tracking parameters from links all happen on your device. The blocking rules are downloaded, but what they match against is never sent anywhere. You can turn each feature off in Settings, and turn blockers off for a single site.

Declining a cookie banner tells that website you reject its optional cookies, using the site's own banner.

Private tabs

Pages in private tabs aren't added to your history, don't get saved thumbnails and aren't restored when the app reopens. Their cookies and site storage are discarded when the last private tab closes.

Private tabs don't hide you from websites, your network or your internet provider. What you type in a private tab stays on your device until you search or open a page, because suggestions from other services are off. Site icons still load, as the table shows.

Camera, microphone, location and motion

Browsa only uses these so that a website can, after you allow it. You're asked before a site gets access, by Browsa or by iOS, and Browsa remembers its answers on your device. Browsa itself doesn't record, read or store your camera, microphone, location or motion data. What a site does with it is covered by that site's policy. You can also turn access off for Browsa in your device's Settings.

Keeping and deleting your data

Your data stays on your device until you remove it. Tab thumbnails are deleted when their tabs are gone. You can clear your history, cookies and site data in Browsa's Settings and delete downloads in the Files app. Deleting the app deletes everything it stored, including cookies and site data, apart from any copies in your existing device backups. We hold no copy of your data, so there's nothing for us to delete or give you.

Children

Browsa isn't directed at children, and we don't knowingly collect anything from anyone, of any age. It gives access to the open web, and it has no parental controls of its own.

Browsa for Mac

The Mac app is the same browser underneath. Everything above about blocking, link cleaning, search suggestions, private tabs and site permissions applies to it too, and it talks to the same services in the table. These are the differences, and the things only the Mac app does.

The Privacy Pack

The ad and tracker blocker, the cookie banner decliner and Clean Links make up the Privacy Pack in the Library. It's on from the start, works on your Mac as described in Blocking and link cleaning, and each part can be turned off in the Library or in Settings. The Mac app also sends Global Privacy Control to every site, asking it not to sell or share your data. That's on by default too, and you can turn it off in Settings > Privacy.

Extensions

You can add Chrome extensions, and Firefox ones where they work, from the Library. Before one is added, Browsa shows you what it will be able to do, and an update that asks for more waits for your yes. An extension is someone else's software: it can see and change the pages it's allowed to, and it may send what it sees to its developer, under that developer's privacy policy. The extensions marked as tested in the Library have been tried in Browsa by us. That's a check that they work, not a review of their privacy. Running Safari extensions from other apps is an experimental setting, off by default.

Importing from another browser

When you ask it to, Browsa reads bookmarks and history from Chrome, Arc, Edge, Brave, Vivaldi or Safari on your Mac, and from Arc its spaces and tabs. It reads their files directly, only reads them, and keeps what it brings over on your Mac. It never reads passwords or cookies. Safari keeps its files behind macOS's Full Disk Access, so importing from Safari asks you to grant that first, and you can take it away again in System Settings.

The Agent Pack: Agents, Notes and Radar

The Agent Pack is off until you turn it on, during setup or in the Library.

  • Agents lets coding agents such as Claude Code, Codex and Cursor use Browsa. It runs a small server that only answers programs on your Mac (127.0.0.1) holding a private key that Browsa makes. Agents work in their own Agent space, where they can open, read, click through and take screenshots of pages, and they have to ask in the Agents panel before touching one of your own tabs. Browsa doesn't send anything to an AI service itself, but what an agent reads through Browsa goes wherever that agent sends it, under that agent's own terms. Add to Claude Code runs Claude Code's own setup command, which saves Browsa's address and key in Claude Code's settings on your Mac.
  • Notes are notes you leave on pages. They're kept on your Mac, and connected agents can read, reply to and resolve them.
  • Radar finds the development servers running on your Mac. While it's on, Browsa looks every few seconds at which programs on your Mac are listening for connections, which folder they run in and their git branch. It stops looking when you turn Radar off, and none of it leaves your Mac.

Where your data is kept

The Mac app keeps your spaces, tabs, history, notes, extensions and settings in your user Library, in folders named com.orriginal.browsa under Application Support, WebKit and HTTPStorages. Files you download go to your Downloads folder. Nothing is synced between your devices, and Browsa doesn't use iCloud. Time Machine and other backups include these folders, under the terms you set up.

You can clear your history, cookies and site data in Settings > Privacy, or have Browsa clear them each time it quits. On a Mac, moving the app to the Bin doesn't delete what it stored. To remove everything, quit Browsa and delete those folders too.

Crash reports, camera and microphone

Browsa for Mac is downloaded from browsa.app rather than the App Store, so Apple doesn't share crash reports or usage statistics from it with us, and we don't collect them. Websites can only use your camera, microphone or location after you allow them, and macOS asks first as well. You can change that in System Settings > Privacy & Security.

This website

browsa.app is a static site hosted by Cloudflare, which also serves the Mac app's downloads. It sets no cookies and has no analytics. Cloudflare processes your IP address to deliver the pages and protect the site from abuse.

To know roughly how many people use Browsa for Mac, the site counts two things: how many times the app checks for updates, with the app's version number, and how many downloads start. Each count is a number and a version, with no IP address, device details, location or identifier, so it can't be linked to you or to other counts. We view these totals in PostHog, which receives only the count and version from our server, never anything from your browser or Mac. Nothing else about how you use Browsa is counted.
